design · 2015 to 2016

my learning lab

A self-assessment and preference exploration space that helps learners understand their own accessibility needs, set learning goals and reflect on their progress. It was part of the FLOE project at the Inclusive Design Research Centre, OCAD University, Toronto.

Inclusive Design Research Centre, OCAD University · volunteer designer · design team: Dana Ayotte, Sepideh Shahi, Neeta Khanuja · video · project wiki

i.

learners and use cases

We wrote personas and use cases for four kinds of people: beginners starting something new, learners improving a skill they already have, experienced users of the dashboard, and the parents, teachers and peers who support them, with the learner’s permission.

ii.

three connected spaces

I helped define the dashboard’s features around one learner, Jamie. My goals holds goals and milestones, my journal holds notes and moods, and my files holds documents. Each can link to the others: a note to a milestone, a document to a goal.

dashboard features

iii.

information architecture

The home page shows progress, activity, the journal and preferences at a glance. Primary navigation leads to goals, journal and files, each with its own list and detail views.

iv.

a note that can become a goal

In the wireframes, anything a learner writes starts as a note. The dashboard then asks whether to link it to a goal, a preference or the calendar, or to turn it into a goal of its own.

first design draft

v.

from interface to prototype

I designed the interface in Adobe Illustrator and built an interactive prototype in Framer.js, so the team could try the flow from setting a goal to reflecting on it.
