design · [year]

braille touch pad

Touch screens are flat. Braille is not. This self-initiated project designed a tangible layer that sits between a finger and a phone screen, so blind users can type in braille the way they read it.

Algo Design Studio · INSIGHT 2018, National Institute of Design

i.

six keys

The screen is divided into a grid of six, arranged like the six dots of a braille cell. Pressing the right keys types a letter.

We built several keypads on Android to find the usability problems: different key sizes, a space bar and a clear button, and a QWERTY keyboard for comparison.

ii.

a layer you can feel

The first layer was a rubber plate with printed braille over semiconductor foam, with thin aluminium wire so a touch still reaches the screen.

Then a mould made at the CEPT University fabrication lab, Ahmedabad, gave a silicone surface with raised braille.

iii.

testing at L.V. Prasad

We ran an informal test with blind users at L.V. Prasad Eye Institute, Hyderabad. They asked for immediate audio feedback, time to finish a letter, enough space between keys and a way to delete one letter instead of the whole message. Pressing keys one after another felt easier than pressing them together.

iv.

from typing to learning

The tests pointed to a better use: learning braille. In a braille tutor game, a story plays as sound and pauses until the child presses the right letter on the pad.
