NeuroPottery: two minds, one pot

Making things is physical. People living with paralysis, ALS or after a stroke cannot throw a pot, and friends, families and therapy partners who live apart rarely get to make something together. Most brain-computer interfaces move a cursor; they rarely let people create.

NeuroPottery splits pottery between two people in virtual reality. One person’s brain signals turn the wheel, add water and fire the kiln. The other person’s hands shape the clay. Neither can finish the pot alone, and every pot records both of them.

Second place in the BCI programming and arts category at the BR41N.IO Brain-Computer Interface Designers’ Hackathon, organised by g.tec medical engineering at IEEE SMC 2026, Seattle, October 4–5 · team Cracked Pots: Harshiddhi Pathak, Neeta Khanuja and Param Rajpura · HAIx Lab, IIT Gandhinagar, and HCII, Carnegie Mellon

iii.

from brain signal to clay

The EEG streams over LSL to a PC. A Python bridge turns it into focus, calm, blinks and jaw clenches, and sends them ten times a second over Wi-Fi to a Unity 6 app running on the headset.

  1. the Mindg.Nautilus EEG, 16 channels
  2. LSL streamon the PC
  3. Python bridgefocus, calm, blinks, jaw clench
  4. UDP over Wi-Fi10 packets per second
  5. the HandsUnity 6 on VIVE Focus Vision, hand tracking
iv.

reading the mind

Each control comes from a different signal and set of channels. A 30-second calibration learns each person’s own range: 15 seconds relaxed, then 15 seconds counting back from 100 in sevens.

  • focus · betaFz, Cz, F3, F4. An engagement index, β / (α + θ), spins the wheel from 0 to 150 RPM. Beta stays under 25 Hz to keep out muscle noise.
  • calm · alphaOz, PO7, PO8. Alpha (8–12 Hz) as a share of total power decides the glaze in the kiln.
  • jaw clench · muscleT7, T8. Adds 15% water. Focus is held during a clench, so water can’t fake focus.
  • 2–3 blinks · frontalFp1, Fp2. Gaps under 0.8 s move to the next phase: throw, fire, new pot. Single natural blinks are ignored.
v.

Clay dries while the Hands work. Below 30% moisture, the pot will crack in the kiln, so the Mind has to keep it wet.

vii.

problems solved along the way

Every detector was validated on synthetic EEG before the live demo.

  • VR streaming blockedThe PC’s Intel graphics could not stream to the headset, so the app runs as a standalone Android build on it.
  • no LSL on AndroidThe Python bridge sends processed values to the headset over UDP.
  • muscle noise in betaCentral channels and beta capped at 25 Hz keep jaw and face muscles out of the focus signal.
  • a clench could fake focusFocus is held during a clench and for 2–3 seconds after.
  • clay that explodedTwo hands at once blew the clay apart. Exact clay volume, speed limits and resistance fixed it.
  • validationThe focus index rose from 0.08 at low beta to 1.96 at high beta. Holding focus cut clench noise from 0.36 to 0.10. All 11 natural blinks were ignored, and clay volume stayed within 1%.

Focus shapes it. Calm fires it. Two minds, one pot.
