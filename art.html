art

My practice is grounded in improvisation, with meaning emerging through visual art, movement, space, and human behavior. I often begin with emotions and observations from everyday life. Through figures, gestures, and relationships, I explore experiences of connection, tension, vulnerability, and reflection.

improvisation & interactive art

I often begin without knowing what the final image will become. In the interactive work, improvisation extends beyond my own hand, allowing movement, technology, and participation to shape the work as it unfolds.

drawing with both hands

Two pens, one in each hand, drawing at the same time.

ambidextrous motion

@ Urmila Art Gallery, Kanoria Centre for Arts

Drawing with both hands introduces a degree of unpredictability. Sensors worn on each hand capture the movement and translate it into a live digital trace, allowing the physical and digital drawings to unfold together.

Participants engaged with the ambidextrous drawing setup to create both physical drawings and digital traces. Shown together, these outcomes reveal how the same gesture can appear as a mark on paper and as a movement-based visualization.

lost in place

@ Bauhaus-Universität Weimar

Lost in Place is an interactive installation that uses virtual fog as a metaphor for disorientation and fading familiarity. As viewers move, the fog clears to reveal their image, then gradually returns, creating an unstable experience of seeing and losing oneself.

Built with live camera capture, motion tracking, projection, and Processing. With Lilly Chan · MinD 2019

synergy

@ 1399 Gallery, Lisbon

Figures emerge through contrast, but the spaces between them matter just as much. I became interested in how bodies can appear separate, connected, crowded, or held together within the same composition.

Shown as part of my solo exhibition Where We Need to Be at 1399 Gallery in Lisbon. The exhibition brought together works exploring identity, connection, adaptation, and how we understand ourselves through others.

body, movement and space

Movement gives me a way to work through gesture, rhythm, and emotion. These studies begin with improvised movement and develop into compositions where the body, image, and surrounding space come together.

Through improvisation, I explore how the body responds to space, constraint, and possibility. Gesture and posture create shifting relationships between the body and its surroundings.
